What AIPAC said

On October 21, 2026, AIPAC announced it would stop issuing endorsements, making direct PAC contributions and earmarking funds for candidates. It said it would focus on increasing organized giving by individuals and on newer fundraising tools. AIPAC confirmed to NPR that its affiliated super PAC, the United Democracy Project (UDP), will keep operating.[[s:3]] A spokesperson said the group's mission and strategy have not changed.[[s:3]]

That matters for how to read what follows. The announcement did not say UDP would stop spending, and it described a change going forward. The question this report asks is narrower: what do the filings show, and what do they not?

The reported total moved by $398,683.44

The FEC's totals for UDP (committee C00799031), as of its pre-general report covering the period through October 14, show $53,603,927.44 in independent expenditures this cycle.[[s:1]] Independent expenditures are spending on ads, mail and messages that urge a vote for or against a candidate, made without coordinating with the campaign.

After a report closes, a committee has to file 24-hour or 48-hour notices for new expenditures. We pulled every notice UDP has filed in the 2026 cycle on October 28 and added those dated after October 14.[[s:2]] There are five, all opposing Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic nominee in the Michigan Senate race. Together they come to $398,683.44, which brings the running total to $54,002,610.88, or about $54.0 million.

Not all of the increase can be placed relative to the announcement. $189,240.72 is dated October 15 and 19, before it. $94,620.36 is dated October 23, after it. The other $114,822.36 is dated October 21, the day of the announcement, and the FEC rows carry a date, not a time, so we cannot say whether those two expenditures came before or after it. If both count as on or after the announcement, the total is $209,442.72, 52.5 percent of the increase. Either way, it is spending that began before the announcement and continued across it.

The same payee, Riviera LLC, appears on direct mail in the weeks before. Our pull also shows $94,620.36 mailers dated October 7 and October 13, which were already in the October 14 total. The filings describe each item as "direct mail" or "text messaging".

One note on the arithmetic. A tracker we have not identified put UDP's cumulative figure at about $54.0 million through October 26. The sum above reaches the same figure by adding the five notices to the FEC's October 14 total, and the FEC shows no UDP independent expenditure dated after October 23 as of our pull.

Four kinds of money, kept apart

Several numbers circulate in this story, and they measure different things.

  • Independent expenditures are what UDP spends itself on communications, such as the $53.6 million above.
  • Contributions from a committee to others. NPR, citing FEC filings through September 30, reported that UDP contributed about $13 million to candidates and committees.[[s:3]] The FEC's totals show $13,131,200 in a field labeled as contributions to federal candidate committees, a category we have not tried to resolve here.[[s:1]]
  • Total disbursements, $85,057,423.26 through October 14, include everything the committee paid out, including the independent expenditures. Adding it to the first two figures would count the same dollars twice.[[s:1]]
  • Individual giving by AIPAC members. A spokesperson told NPR that members gave more than $90 million directly to candidates this cycle. NPR noted that AIPAC's own FEC figures show nearly $25 million in contributions to candidates and committees in 2026 through September 30.[[s:3]] Individual contributions are made by people, not spent by UDP.

The reported $9 million

Separately, the New York Times reported on October 21 that UDP would send up to $9 million to the Great Lakes Conservatives Fund, a super PAC supporting Republican Mike Rogers, citing two people familiar with the plans. Newsweek, relaying the account, said the money reportedly went out after the October 14 reporting deadline and might not appear in public filings until after Election Day on November 3. It said it emailed AIPAC, the fund and both campaigns and reported no reply.[[s:4]]

It is a different kind of money from the $398,683.44. A transfer to another committee is not UDP's own independent expenditure, and what the recipient does with it would be that committee's spending to report. We do not add the two figures and do not describe $9 million as verified spending.

The race

Abdul El-Sayed is the Democratic nominee and Mike Rogers the Republican in the Michigan U.S. Senate race. Both have TIP profiles: Abdul El-Sayed and Mike Rogers. NPR reported that several of UDP's recent independent expenditures oppose El-Sayed.[[s:3]]
