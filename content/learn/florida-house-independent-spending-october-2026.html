What was measured

A campaign can raise millions of dollars and still have other organizations spend millions more to influence its election. Independent expenditures are spending by groups that are not the candidate's campaign, on communications that support or oppose a candidate. They are reported to the FEC, in many cases within 24 or 48 hours. They are not donations to a campaign and they are not the same as the totals an ad-tracking company estimates.[[s:1]]

We built this report from those FEC filings. The Washington Post reported advertising figures for these two districts in early October, drawn from the tracker AdImpact; we could not retrieve the article and have not used its numbers.[[s:2]]

[[el:spend14]] [[el:spend25]]

Bars show independent expenditures reported on 24/48-hour reports, by the date the communication was disseminated, per week starting on the date shown. Spending that supports one nominee or opposes the other is grouped together. The same transaction also appears on later periodic filings; we count it once. Third-party and write-in candidates in these districts have no reported independent expenditures.[[s:1]]

The candidates and their own money

Candidate committees report separately. Kathy Castor (D) had reported [[v:castor]] in receipts through [[v:castor_d]], and Mike Beltran (R) [[v:beltran]] through [[v:beltran_d]]. In FL-25, Jared Moskowitz (D) had reported [[v:moskowitz]] through [[v:moskowitz_d]] and Scott Singer (R) [[v:singer]] through [[v:singer_d]].[[s:3]] Those are the campaigns' own receipts. They are not added to the independent expenditures above, and the periods are not identical.

Each district also has third-party and write-in candidates on the state's qualified list. All of them appear on the FL-14 and FL-25 pages, along with each candidate's TIP Issue Grades where they exist. Money cannot stand in for policy: where a candidate has a congressional record, TIP's six-issue grades show what that record says.

Does the spending change the race?

The filings show where money was being deployed. They do not show whether it changed anyone's mind or the outcome. Judging effect would take comparable polling over time, and we have not found it for these districts. Kalshi lists contracts on both races, and those prices are market-implied estimates, not measures of effect.[[s:4]]
