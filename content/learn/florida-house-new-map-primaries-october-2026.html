The races at a glance

Florida elects 28 members of the U.S. House on November 3 under a map the legislature passed in a special session this spring and Gov. Ron DeSantis signed in May. WLRN and WUSF report that 21 of the 28 districts were reworked and districts 1 to 7 were left alone; WUSF attributes the count to the News Service of Florida.[[s:1]][[s:2]] The Division of Elections lists 83 House candidates on the November ballot.[[s:7]] Early voting runs October 24 through 31 in all 67 counties, and some counties offer extra days, according to the Florida Department of State.[[s:10]]

[[el:stats_house]]

What the new map changed

Reporters estimate how the 2024 presidential vote would have split on the new lines. In the redrawn FL-14, which moved away from most of Tampa and into rural Hillsborough County, they put Donald Trump ahead 55 to 44; Democrat Kathy Castor said she would run there. In FL-9, held by Democrat Darren Soto, they put Trump ahead 58 to 41.[[s:1]][[s:2]]

[[el:map_story]] [[el:trump_bars]]

A voting rights group sued in Leon County Circuit Court soon after the governor signed the bill. The Florida Supreme Court declined an emergency request to block the map, so the new lines govern the 2026 elections while the case moves through the lower court.[[s:1]][[s:2]] The official boundaries are the legislature's plan EOGPCRP2026.[[s:8]] The margins above are the reporters' figures for those lines; we have not recomputed them, and WUSF attributes the district count to the News Service of Florida, so the two articles may not be independent confirmation.

Castor and Soto

Castor faces Republican Mike Beltran in FL-14. Soto is on the ballot in FL-9.[[s:7]] Both Democrats are sitting members with recorded congressional acts, and TIP grades both on five of its six issues, with the Citizens United reform issue not graded for either.[[s:9]]

Three primaries under the new lines

On August 18, Rep. Jared Moskowitz won the Democratic primary in the redrawn FL-25 over Oliver Larkin, a challenger backed by the Democratic Socialists of America, Reuters reported. The wire copy gave no vote shares.[[s:3]] Algemeiner reported that Moskowitz won [[v:mosk_pct]] percent. In FL-6, it reported Rep. Randy Fine won the Republican primary with [[v:fine_pct]] percent to Dan Bilzerian's [[v:bilz_pct]] percent. The Division of Elections roster records Moskowitz and Fine as winners.[[s:4]][[s:7]]

[[el:result_bars]]

The shares come from a single outlet whose framing of these primaries is interpretive; we use its numbers only and have not checked them against the Division of Elections counts. Moskowitz faces Republican Scott Singer in November.[[s:7]]

FL-20: a new district for Wasserman Schultz

Rep. Debbie Wasserman Schultz had represented the 25th District, which the new map made more Republican-leaning, and she ran in the 20th instead, Ballotpedia reported.[[s:5]] The roster records her as the August 18 winner, and Algemeiner reported she won [[v:dws_pct]] percent in a crowded field without giving the others' shares.[[s:4]][[s:7]] Sheila Cherfilus-McCormick, who had held the 20th, resigned in April 2026, Ballotpedia reported; she is among the defeated primary candidates on the roster.[[s:5]][[s:7]]

TIP records for three of the winners

TIP grades only where a candidate has recorded congressional acts. All three below are sitting members, so each has grades, but not on every issue. Moskowitz has five graded issues, including F- on the data bill of rights and war-crime accountability and A+ on three others. Fine has three: F- on war-crime accountability, universal healthcare and the US-Israel military merger provision, with none of his recorded acts aligned. Wasserman Schultz has four: F- on the data bill and war-crime accountability, A+ on universal healthcare and the merger provision. The rest are not graded, which is not a zero.[[s:9]]

What we do not know

We do not have the Division of Elections vote counts for the three primaries, and the shares above should be read as reported. We do not know the plaintiff or the schedule of the Leon County case. We have not recomputed the reported presidential margins from precinct results. This article does not describe the criminal case involving Cherfilus-McCormick because we have not reviewed the court record. Figures are as of October 24, 2026.
