The race at a glance

Florida elects a U.S. senator on November 3 in a special election. Sen. Ashley Moody, a Republican, was appointed in January 2025 to replace Marco Rubio after he became secretary of state. She faces Democratic state Rep. Angie Nixon of Jacksonville and Neil J. Gillespie, who has no party affiliation. The winner serves the rest of Rubio's term, about two years, and has to run again in 2028.[[s:4]][[s:5]][[s:8]] Early voting runs October 24 through 31 in all 67 counties, and some counties offer extra days, according to the Florida Department of State.[[s:10]]

The newest poll

The University of North Florida's Public Opinion Research Lab polled [[v:sen_n]] likely midterm voters by telephone from October 12 to 19. It found Moody at [[v:sen_moody]] percent and Nixon at [[v:sen_nixon]] percent, with [[v:sen_undec]] percent undecided and a margin of error of ±[[v:sen_moe]] points.[[s:1]][[s:2]]

[[el:poll_unf]]

WLRN reports that the lab's July poll had Moody ahead by [[v:sen_july]] points. Action News Jax confirms the October numbers but gives no July Senate figure, so that comparison rests on a single report.[[s:1]][[s:2]] Sean Freeder, a UNF political scientist and the lab's faculty director, pointed to national factors in midterm elections when he discussed the results. That is his reading, not something the poll measured.[[s:1]][[s:2]]

Earlier polls disagreed

Three polls of likely Florida voters in September found anything from a tie to a seven-point lead for Moody. After two of them, Moody's campaign described the race to supporters as a "dead heat."[[s:3]]

[[el:sept_polls]]

The pollsters, field dates and sample sizes differ, which is a reason not to read any one number as the state of the race. We do not average them.

No debate so far

Earlier in September, Moody declined to debate Nixon. She told Action News Jax she would instead draw attention to Nixon being a "self-proclaimed socialist," the News Service of Florida reported.[[s:4]] On October 1, Florida Democratic Party chair Nikki Fried and Rep. Gallop Franklin II said Moody was avoiding reporters and town halls, and Fried tied her to the Hope Florida controversy. Moody told the News Service that she would keep fighting for constituents in Washington and keep engaging with voters.[[s:4]]

Debates were also an issue in the Democratic primary: WFLX reported before the vote that Alex Vindman had declined to debate Nixon.[[s:5]]

How Nixon got here

Nixon won the August 18 Democratic primary over Vindman. The Division of Elections roster records her as the winner and Vindman as defeated, and Algemeiner reported the result without vote shares.[[s:6]][[s:8]] Before the vote, WFLX reported that Vindman had raised roughly [[v:vindman_raised]] through June, according to the latest campaign finance totals it cited. It gave no comparable figure for Nixon, who it described as stressing her Florida roots and a working-class message on wages and affordability.[[s:5]]

The candidates and their TIP records

The Issues Party grades candidates on six issues, but only where they have recorded congressional acts on that issue. Moody, who has served in the Senate since 2025, has two graded issues: war-crime accountability and universal healthcare. TIP shows an F- on both, with none of her 9 recorded acts on the first and none of 3 on the second aligned with TIP's position. Her other four issues are not graded.[[s:9]]

Nixon is a state legislator with no recorded congressional acts, so all six of her issues are not graded. Gillespie's are also not graded. How the grades work is explained on our methods page.
