Two numbers

To get on the Democratic primary ballot in Illinois' 4th Congressional District, a candidate needed 697 valid signatures. To get on the November ballot as an independent, a candidate needed 10,816, more than 15 times as many.[[s:1]]

Thresholds as reported by the Riverside-Brookfield Landmark. The ratio is LLF arithmetic: 10,816 divided by 697 is 15.5.[[s:1]]

How the ballot was set

  • October 27, 2025. Rep. García filed petitions for another term.[[s:2]]
  • Shortly before the 5 p.m. deadline on November 3. His then chief of staff, Patty García, who is not related to him, filed to run. He later withdrew his own petitions.[[s:2]]
  • November 6, 2025. García explained his decision to ABC7 Chicago (see below).[[s:3]]
  • March 2026 primary. Patty García was the only Democrat on the ballot.[[s:2]]
  • July 21, 2026. The Illinois State Board of Elections voted 8-0 to remove independents Ald. Byron Sigcho-Lopez and Mayra Macías from the November ballot.[[s:1]]
  • August 4, 2026. Sigcho-Lopez filed a lawsuit seeking a place on the ballot and challenging the independent signature requirement. He said his team collected more than 17,000 signatures. The Board said it does not comment on pending litigation.[[s:4]] Ballot Access News separately reported a federal suit filed July 31 against Illinois ballot access laws for independent and minor party U.S. House candidates.[[s:5]]

The removals

Hearing officers found Macías 486 valid signatures short of 10,816 and Sigcho-Lopez 1,222 short, and the Board adopted those findings 8-0, the Landmark reported. Supporters of the Democratic nominee, Patty García, had challenged many of the signatures.[[s:1]] Macías and Sigcho-Lopez called the outcome disenfranchisement and "rigged."[[s:1]] We found no response to the removals from Patty García's campaign, which said it had raised more than $80,000 in the second quarter and over $350,000 since she declared.[[s:1]]

Signature totals that candidates say they turned in differ from one source to another. We use only the Board's counts of valid signatures and shortfalls.

The congressman's explanation

García told ABC7 Chicago his cardiologist advised him to step aside and that his wife is fighting multiple sclerosis. He said the timing reflected family obligations, not the Chicago political machine, that he did not want to be seen as a king anointing a successor and that anyone could have filed.[[s:3]] Critics called the move a coronation and a Chicago machine play, WBEZ reported, without naming them.[[s:2]] Both are their characterizations or explanations, not findings of this report. Nothing we found says the handoff, the signature challenges or the removals were coordinated.

The November ballot

Four names are on the November ballot in the Illinois 4th District race: Patty García (Democrat), Lupe Castillo (Republican), Chris Getty, the Lyons village president, as an independent and Ed Hershey of the Working Class Party.[[s:2]] The district's current member, Chuy García, is not on the ballot.
