Who won

Juliana Stratton, the lieutenant governor, won the March 17 Democratic primary for the U.S. Senate seat Dick Durbin is leaving. With 82 percent of precincts reporting, Capitol News Illinois had her at 39.6 percent, Raja Krishnamoorthi at 33.6 and Robin Kelly at 18.5. The Associated Press called the race at 9:40 p.m. and Krishnamoorthi conceded.[[s:2]] She faces Republican Don Tracy, the former state party chair, on November 3 in the Illinois U.S. Senate race.[[s:2]] The shares above are from a partial count, not the certified result.

Stratton and Tracy have no graded issues on TIP: neither has served in Congress, and a candidate who is not graded has not scored zero.

Three kinds of money

AdImpact, which tracks advertising, counted $55.3 million in Senate primary ad spending and reservations as of March 13 and called it the most expensive Senate primary in Illinois on record.[[s:1]] The total hides who paid. The table separates what each candidate's own campaign spent, what outside groups spent for the candidate and what outside groups spent against.

The campaign that spent the most

Capitol News Illinois reported that Krishnamoorthi had more than $19 million in the bank when he entered the race in May 2025 and spent more than $27 million on advertising.[[s:2]] On AdImpact's count his $28.9 million was nearly all campaign money. Stratton's own campaign accounted for $1.1 million of her $15.5 million, about 7 percent, by the arithmetic of AdImpact's figures.[[s:1]]

The PAC behind the winner

AdImpact attributed $14.4 million to the Illinois Future PAC, supporting Stratton.[[s:1]] Pritzker endorsed her, and Capitol News Illinois said he largely bankrolled the PAC.[[s:2]] The American Prospect, in a report published the day of the primary, wrote that Pritzker put at least $5 million of his own money toward her campaign.[[s:3]]

The money against her

Fairshake is a crypto-industry super PAC. AdImpact counted $9.0 million from it attacking Stratton.[[s:1]] The American Prospect said almost $10 million.[[s:3]] Capitol News Illinois said crypto-linked PACs spent more than $10 million against her, and a Chicago Sun-Times count of more than $5.5 million in anti-Stratton ads appeared only in a search summary we did not open.[[s:2]] The totals differ because the outlets count different things on different dates, so the honest range is about $5.5 million to more than $10 million. The Prospect also reported smaller amounts from Protect Progress: $163,639 against Stratton, $277,000 for Kelly and $30,183 for Krishnamoorthi.[[s:3]]

Stratton told reporters before the primary that she had not accepted money from the AIPAC PAC and did not say whether she would take money from an affiliated group, the Prospect reported.[[s:3]]
