Outside committees reported $10,135,610.20 opposing Republican Mike Bouchard and $4,164,104.66 opposing Democrat Christina Hines in Michigan's 10th District, in entries disseminated through October 24, 2026. HMP, as the FEC lists the committee, accounts for $7,212,674.95 of the money reported against Bouchard.[[s:1]][[s:2]]

The FEC's own by-candidate total for HMP against him is $175,718.76. Both numbers are correct. The smaller one adds up four entries in periodic reports, dated September 15 and 22. The larger one includes the 24-hour notices through October 24. Neither means much without its dates.[[s:2]][[s:3]]

What outside committees reported

These are independent expenditures, reported to the FEC on Schedule E. We retrieved them on October 28. Support and opposition are separate lines for each candidate:

  • Supporting Hines: $2,537,611.79.
  • Opposing Hines: $4,164,104.66.
  • Supporting Bouchard: $918,413.55.
  • Opposing Bouchard: $10,135,610.20.

The lines should not be added into one pool. The same ad can be reported against one candidate and not for the other, and outside spending is not campaign money: none of it went to either campaign.[[s:1]]

The estimates behind HMP's figure

HMP reported its opposition to Bouchard in 23 entries disseminated from September 15 through October 24. Three of them are television advertising notices paid to Waterfront Strategies Inc., each labeled "ESTIMATE" by the filer: $1,553,580.00 on October 6, $1,655,618.40 on October 13 and $2,752,423.20 on October 20. Together they come to $5,961,621.60, about 83 percent of HMP's total against him.[[s:2]][[s:4]]

The label matters. These are amounts the filer reported as estimates, and later reports can change them. A notice that describes airtime does not show that the airtime ran, or how many people saw it.[[s:4]]

How the reporting calendar changes the picture

Committees report independent expenditures two ways. A 24-hour or 48-hour notice, filed soon after the spending, carries each payment as it happens. A periodic report, filed on a monthly or quarterly schedule, repeats payments for its period. The FEC's by-candidate roll-up is built from periodic reports, so it stops at each committee's last one.[[s:3]]

That is why the two HMP figures differ. We compared the roll-up with the sum of the periodic-report entries in all 13 committee-and-side lines for this district, and they match to the cent. The roll-up stops at late September for most committees and at July for Honor, Duty and Discipline PAC and Pro-Choice Majority Action. Notices cover the weeks after.[[s:3]]

Adding every notice to every periodic-report entry would count some payments twice, because a committee can give the same payment different transaction numbers on the two forms, or date it a day apart. We paired each periodic-report entry with at most one notice from the same committee for the same side, to the same cent, within seven days, and kept the notice. Without that step the totals would have been about $2.8 million supporting Hines and $10.3 million opposing Bouchard.[[s:3]]

Who else reported

The FEC lists most of these committees as hybrid PACs. HMP also reported $310,296.80 supporting Hines. Giffords PAC reported $495,191.89 supporting Hines and $472,065.90 opposing Bouchard. Pro-Choice Majority Action reported $499,999.87 supporting Hines, CFFE PAC $279,679.91 supporting her and Ready for the Fight $563,901.80 opposing Bouchard.[[s:2]][[s:5]]

CLF, also a hybrid PAC, reported $2,659,832.86 opposing Hines in five periodic-report entries dated September 23 through 30. Our pull has no later CLF entry, so that figure is not necessarily its total.[[s:2]][[s:5]]

Among committees the FEC lists as Super PACs, Restoration of America PAC reported $1,059,150.00 opposing Hines in notices dated October 6. LCV Victory Fund reported $564,922.84 opposing Bouchard. No Going Back PAC Inc. reported $372,461.54 supporting Bouchard and $206,626.19 opposing Hines, and Honor, Duty and Discipline PAC reported $352,983.87 supporting Bouchard.[[s:2]][[s:5]]

We have not described any committee's alignment. The FEC's records list no party or movement for them, and this account follows each filing's support or oppose indicator.

The candidates' own committees

These are the campaigns' reported finances, separate from the outside spending above. Through October 14, 2026, Hines's committee, Christina for Congress, reported $4,159,692.85 in receipts and $3,152,065.09 in disbursements, with $1,007,627.76 on hand.[[s:6]]

Bouchard's principal committee, Friends of Michael Bouchard Inc, reported $2,672,721.59 in receipts and $1,112,737.40 in disbursements through the same date, with $1,559,984.19 on hand, in a pre-general report filed October 22. He has three smaller authorized nominee funds whose figures are not added here. One of them, the EMB Nominee Fund, filed a termination report that the FEC received on October 13. That is a different committee from his principal committee, and it does not mean his campaign ended.[[s:6]]

What this does not show

  • It does not show why any committee spent or who paid for the spending.
  • It does not show that any ad ran or what it said. Notice amounts are the filer's own estimates.
  • It does not show whether any committee coordinated with a campaign.
  • It does not show that any of the spending changed a vote.

The open seat

The seat is open because Rep. John James is running for governor. Hines and Bouchard are the Democratic and Republican nominees; the Michigan Department of State's listing also shows Cubby Nkromo (Green), Mike Saliba (Libertarian) and Andrea Kirby (Working Class Party).[[s:7]] We found no poll and no prediction-market contract for this district.
