The result

In the August 4, 2026 Democratic primary for Michigan's 13th District, Donavan McKinney defeated Rep. Shri Thanedar. WDIV's final table, published August 5, lists McKinney at 57,714 votes, or 52 percent, and Thanedar at 53,494, or 48 percent, with all 346 precincts reporting. WDIV said the Associated Press projected McKinney the winner.[[s:1]] Bridge Michigan also reported the AP call and noted that Thanedar was first elected in 2022.[[s:2]]

What outside committees reported

Independent expenditures are payments by a committee that is not the candidate's own, made to support or oppose a candidate without coordinating with the campaign. They are reported to the FEC on Schedule E. We retrieved every entry for both candidates on October 31. The entries run from January 8 through August 4, 2026 by dissemination date, and 99.8 percent of the dollars were disseminated in June and July.[[s:3]]

  • Supporting McKinney: $1,727,586.94.
  • Opposing McKinney: $268,690.00.
  • Supporting Thanedar: $1,848,181.00.
  • Opposing Thanedar: $463,165.48.

Support and opposition are separate lines. None of it went to either campaign.[[s:3]]

How we counted: when a committee reported the same payment on both a 24-hour notice and a later periodic report, we kept one. We also dropped memo entries, which repeat a line already reported. A few small periodic-report entries for McKinney carry payment dates after the primary, from August 10 to September 15. They total $60,363.01, all in support, and were disseminated on or before August 4, so they are included.[[s:3]]

Who reported

The largest reporter was Protect Progress, which the FEC lists as a Super PAC that makes only independent expenditures. It reported $1,774,655.00 supporting Thanedar in four entries dated July 18 through July 28, and $268,690.00 opposing McKinney in five entries dated July 17 through July 29. Those two lines come to $2,043,345.00 for the committee. Its support for Thanedar is 96 percent of the support reported for him.[[s:3]][[s:7]]

Justice Democrats PAC, which the FEC lists as a hybrid PAC, reported $460,546.70 supporting McKinney in 12 entries and $428,888.39 opposing Thanedar in 11, from June 22 through July 28.[[s:3]][[s:7]] Working Families Party PAC, also a hybrid PAC, reported $427,788.40 supporting McKinney in 22 entries from June 23 through August 4. FEC's own by-candidate roll-up shows $297,869.11 for that committee. The roll-up counts only what a committee's periodic reports have recorded so far, so it trails the 24-hour notices. For this committee it equals the sum of its periodic-report entries.[[s:3]][[s:7]]

Two more Super PACs reported support for McKinney: National Nurses United for Patient Protection, $400,000.00 in a single entry on June 18, and American Priorities (AP), $150,000.00 in two entries on July 28 and 31. Thirteen other committees reported smaller amounts on McKinney's side. On Thanedar's side, four others reported smaller amounts: Healthcare for Action PAC, $62,584.00, and the National Association of Realtors PAC, $10,942.00, in support; PAF, $20,000.00, and Emgage Federal PAC, $14,277.09, in opposition.[[s:3]][[s:7]]

The FEC records list no party or movement for these committees, and we did not open a source that says who funds them. The entries say whom each committee supported or opposed, and that is all this account reports.

No entry from the United Democracy Project

The United Democracy Project, a super PAC, has no Schedule E entry for either candidate in the records we pulled.[[s:3]] That covers independent expenditures only. It says nothing about other kinds of activity, and we make no wider claim about AIPAC in this race. For the committee's Michigan spending elsewhere, see our report on its filings in the Senate race.

The candidates' own committees

These are the campaigns' reported finances, a separate matter from the outside spending above, and their reporting periods differ.

McKinney's committee reported cycle-to-date receipts of $1,876,779.88 and disbursements of $1,747,106.34 through October 14, 2026, with $129,673.54 on hand. That period includes the weeks after the primary.[[s:4]]

Thanedar's committee reported cycle-to-date receipts of $3,194,222.05 and disbursements of $4,163,399.35 through September 30, 2026, with $5,935,390.40 on hand. The FEC totals also show $2,970,000.00 in loans the candidate made to his committee this cycle.[[s:5]]

The general election

McKinney is the Democratic nominee in November. The Michigan Department of State's listing shows the Republican nominee as T.P. Nykoriak, listed as "Nykoriak, T.P." The FEC records we pulled show no independent expenditures for or against him.[[s:6]]
