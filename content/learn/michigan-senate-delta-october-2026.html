The market movement

Kalshi lists a contract on Michigan's Senate race: whether a Democrat will be sworn in as senator. The El-Sayed contract is that Democratic-win contract. It pays $1 if the Democrat wins and nothing if not, so its price in cents is what traders were willing to pay for that outcome at that moment. It is a market-implied estimate. It is not a vote share, a poll or a forecast.[[s:1]]

What the timeline shows

Kalshi's own October 9 story said the price fell from 73% to 67% after the first debate between Democrat Abdul El-Sayed and Republican Mike Rogers.[[s:2]] The debate was Thursday, October 8, at 7 p.m. at WOOD-TV in Grand Rapids.[[s:3]] The market's recorded history, pulled from Kalshi's public API, shows when the cents actually came off. All times are Eastern.

  • October 5: the contract closed the day at 73¢.
  • October 6 and 7: closes of 71¢ both days, two cents down before the debate day.
  • October 8, debate day: the price slipped to 69¢ around 1 a.m. and traded between 68¢ and 70¢ through the day. In the hour before the debate it closed at 68¢. In the debate hour, 7 to 8 p.m., it closed at 69¢. The day closed at 69¢.
  • October 9: the contract closed at 67¢, and it was the busiest day of that week: about 140,000 contracts traded, more than three times the debate day's 43,600. The busiest single hour of October 7 to 9, ending 11 a.m., saw about 42,800. The lowest hourly close, 65¢, came in the hour ending 1 p.m.
  • October 10 to 12: 67¢. October 13 to 17: 68¢. October 18: 67¢. October 19 to 22: 69¢.

So the six-cent figure is real if you measure from the October 5 close to the October 9 close. But four to five cents of it was already gone when the debate started, and the price did not move much during the debate itself. The largest trading came the morning after.

What happened around the prices

Reporting on the debate described heated exchanges, with the candidates often talking over each other.[[s:4]] Kalshi's October 9 story tied the decline to that debate and cited reporting by NBC News and the New York Times on one of the exchanges.[[s:2]] Those are reasonable facts to place on the same timeline. The same days also include polling, ad spending and other news, and a price can move on thin order books as easily as on news.

We have no source that explains the October 9 move. In this report it is labeled unexplained.

Markets versus polling

A poll and a market price measure different things. The Marist poll, fielded October 15 to 18, found El-Sayed at 50% and Rogers at 48% among likely voters, with a margin of error of ±4.3 points, and El-Sayed ahead by seven points among registered voters (±4.0).[[s:5]] On October 21 Kalshi quoted the El-Sayed contract at 69¢ and described it as close to its post-debate level.[[s:6]] A 69¢ contract does not mean a 69% vote share. A two-point likely-voter lead is inside that poll's margin of error.

What the market cannot tell us

The contract resolves on the party of the senator sworn in for the term beginning in 2027, so it prices party control of the seat, not the two named candidates' vote totals.[[s:1]] It cannot tell us what voters think about TIP's six issues. That record has to come from votes, documents and statements. Rogers served in the U.S. House, but TIP has no grades for him today, and none for El-Sayed, who has not served in Congress.

Key takeaways

  1. The El-Sayed contract's close fell six cents between October 5 and October 9.
  2. Four to five cents of that was already gone when the debate began; the debate hour itself was quiet.
  3. By October 19 the contract had recovered to 69¢ and has held there through October 22.

Campaign money

El-Sayed's committee had reported $45.2 million in receipts through September 30. Rogers's committee had reported $23.0 million through October 22.[[s:7]] Those are candidate committee receipts.

What to watch next

The next FEC pre-election reports, any new Michigan polling and the market's volume and spread. When we publish the next Delta Report we will compare the same contract, the same quote method and the same time windows.
