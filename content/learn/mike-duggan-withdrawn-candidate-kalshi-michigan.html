The campaign ended. The contract remained.

Bridge Michigan reported on May 21, 2026 that Mike Duggan had ended his independent campaign in the Michigan governor race.[[s:1]] By October 28, the Michigan Department of State's general-election listing contained five governor tickets and no independent.[[s:2]] Yet a Kalshi contract still carried his name, with its last-trade field showing 0.3 cents.[[s:3]]

The small price is the eye-catching detail. The more useful detail is the wording underneath it: the Duggan contract asks whether he is inaugurated as governor, while the Democratic and Republican contracts ask whether someone from the relevant party is inaugurated.[[s:3]] A reader looking only at the names would miss that difference.

Here, understanding what the price describes starts with separating the campaign's status, the state's candidate listing and the contract's resolution rule.

What Duggan said when he left

Bridge Michigan's Simon D. Schuster reported Duggan's withdrawal on May 21.[[s:1]] The report quoted his statement: “If we were even in the polls and behind in fundraising, we have a path to winning,” he said. “If we were behind in the polls and even in fundraising, we have a path. But we're behind in both.”[[s:1]]

That is Duggan's assessment of his campaign at the time, as reported by Bridge.[[s:1]] It should stay attached to its date and speaker. It supplies no explanation for the market's continuing listing.

The state's list has five tickets

For the November 3, 2026 general election, the Department of State listing read on October 28 shows these governor and lieutenant governor tickets:[[s:2]]

  • Jocelyn Benson and Winnie Brinks — Democratic.[[s:2]]
  • John James and Jay DeBoyer — Republican.[[s:2]]
  • Anthony Hudson and Beau Parmenter — Libertarian.[[s:2]]
  • Donna Brandenburg and Robert Donald Cowper II — U.S. Taxpayers.[[s:2]]
  • Douglas Campbell and Bobbie Clay — Green.[[s:2]]

Mike Duggan does not appear among the governor tickets, and the governor listing contains no independent ticket.[[s:2]] That is the specific record used here.

What the market capture actually says

All market figures below come from the same Kalshi public API capture: October 28, 2026 at 20:21:35 UTC, for the event “Michigan Governor winner?”[[s:3]]

The Duggan contract, GOVPARTYMI-26-MD, was marked active. Its YES bid was 0.0 cents, its YES ask was 0.1 cent and its last-trade field was 0.3 cents.[[s:3]] Those are separately labeled fields; quoting the last trade alone leaves out the bid and ask alongside it.

We describe 0.3 cents as the last-trade value recorded in the snapshot, without claiming a trade occurred at the capture time.

Kalshi also returned all-time volume of 138,350.39 contracts for the Duggan contract.[[s:3]] The unit is contracts, not dollars, and the period is the contract's recorded lifetime. The capture also reported 317.81 contracts traded in the preceding 24 hours, so the contract was not untouched that day. Neither figure establishes when the last trade occurred.

The name and the rule are different layers

The Duggan rule requires Mike Duggan to be inaugurated as Michigan governor pursuant to the 2026 election for the contract to resolve to YES.[[s:3]] It names a person.

The contract labeled Benson, GOVPARTYMI-26-D, instead requires a Democratic-party representative to be inaugurated. Its YES bid was 92.3 cents, its YES ask 93.0 cents and its last-trade value 92.3 cents in the same capture.[[s:3]]

The contract labeled James, GOVPARTYMI-26-R, requires a Republican-party representative to be inaugurated. Its YES bid was 7.1 cents, its YES ask 7.3 cents and its last-trade value 7.5 cents.[[s:3]]

The labels make the contracts look like a candidate comparison, but the rules define different subjects: a named person in one case and party representatives in the others.[[s:3]]
