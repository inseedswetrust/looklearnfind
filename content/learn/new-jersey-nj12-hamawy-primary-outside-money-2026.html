A plurality in a field of 12

Adam Hamawy, an Army veteran and trauma surgeon who served in Gaza, according to JTA, won the Democratic primary for New Jersey's 12th Congressional District, the New Jersey Globe reports. The seat opened when Rep. Bonnie Watson Coleman announced in November 2025 that she would not run again.[[s:7]] The Globe's June 2 projection, made on most of the ballots, had Hamawy at about 28% in a field of 12 Democrats, 14 points ahead of East Brunswick Mayor Brad Cohen.[[s:1]]

JTA reported a 12-point lead over Cohen with 86% of the vote counted.[[s:2]] The two figures come from different points in the count.

The super PAC that spent

Both outlets report spending for Hamawy by American Priorities, a super PAC that JTA says aims to counter AIPAC by backing pro-Palestinian progressives. The Globe puts the spending at close to $1.6 million on ads introducing him to voters. JTA puts it at $2 million.[[s:1]][[s:2]] The gap is unexplained, so we give the range as reported, not a filing total. The Globe also reports that Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez backed Hamawy, and that Watson Coleman stayed neutral in the primary.[[s:1]]

What the reports say about AIPAC

The Globe reports that AIPAC stayed out of this spring's race after spending heavily in a February special primary elsewhere in the state.[[s:1]] JTA's report does not mention any AIPAC or United Democracy Project spending in this race.[[s:2]] The Globe's statement is about this primary, not a finding about every donor. The reports describe outside support for the winner and no reported AIPAC spending against him, which is a different story from an AIPAC-backed candidate losing.

November

Hamawy faces Republican Gregg Mele (most sources spell his first name Gregg; one spells it Greg), Green Party candidate Andres Jinete and Winston Jordan, who is on the ballot under the line "GET MONEY OUT".[[s:6]] The candidates and ballot lines come from the Division of Elections' official list dated September 4, 2026.
