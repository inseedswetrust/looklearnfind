The race at a glance

Outside money is part of the story in Pennsylvania’s 1st District race. How much depends on which dated report a reader opens. The Philadelphia Inquirer reported on October 16, 2026 that the Congressional Leadership Fund redirected $3.6 million to boost Republican Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick against Democrat Bob Harvie.[[s:1]]

Jewish Insider reported on October 17 that CLF put $4.5 million into the race that week, its first spending in the district since 2020. Neither report, as read, explains the difference between those figures. They are two attributed accounts, not separate amounts to add together and not evidence by themselves of an increase between publication dates.[[s:1]][[s:2]]

As of November 1, 2026, the election is two days away. The roster transcribed from The Green Papers lists Fitzpatrick and Harvie; it is a third-party roster, not an official state list.[[s:3]]

The outside advertising reports

The Inquirer’s October 16 account also said the DCCC began a nearly $1 million advertising campaign for Harvie during the week of its report. That is party advertising, not money in Harvie’s own campaign account.[[s:1]]

Jewish Insider’s October 17 account reported a $400,000 digital and streaming advertising campaign by the Republican Jewish Coalition Victory Fund supporting Fitzpatrick and attacking Harvie. That is a separately identified group and advertising effort.[[s:2]][[s:1]]

The distinction is practical: a reader can identify who is reported to be paying for an effort, its direction and the date of the account.[[s:1]][[s:2]]

The campaigns’ own money

Campaign finances are a separate set of figures. The Inquirer reported that Harvie raised about $888,000 and spent $962,000 from July 1 through September 30, 2026, with $721,000 on hand on October 1. These are the outlet’s reported campaign figures, not a new review of the underlying filings.[[s:1]]

The same October 16 report said Fitzpatrick spent $5 million over the summer, mostly on advertising, and had $2.4 million on hand.[[s:1]]

Neither campaign’s own spending should be combined with an outside advertising figure and relabeled as candidate spending. The campaign accounts describe different transactions from the reported interventions by CLF, the DCCC and the Republican Jewish Coalition Victory Fund. Keeping the accounts separate is more informative than producing a larger but undefined total.[[s:1]][[s:2]]

Read the issue record separately

TIP gives Fitzpatrick A+ grades for Congressional Stock Trading Ban and Universal Healthcare. It gives him F- grades for Data Bill of Rights, War-Crime Accountability and US-Israel Military Merger. Citizens United Reform is “Not graded.” These are TIP’s published issue labels and grades, not an LLF composite rating.[[s:5]]

TIP shows that Harvie has no congressional record and all six issues are not graded. Nor does missing grading information establish agreement or disagreement on an issue.[[s:6]][[s:5]]

Open Fitzpatrick’s TIP profile and Harvie’s TIP profile to inspect the records. The issue account answers a different question from who paid for advertising; neither supplies an explanation for the other.[[s:5]][[s:6]][[s:1]][[s:2]]
