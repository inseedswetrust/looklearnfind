Start with the plain part. For centuries, European mapmakers labeled a huge stretch of Central and Northern Asia "Tartary." The word sits on printed atlases, in travelers' accounts, and in encyclopedias.[[s:1]] It was a geographic label, often a vague one, not the name of a single country.

Why a vague label invites big stories

A name that was never precisely defined is easy to stretch. Online, "Tartaria" now often stands for a far larger claim: that a hidden, advanced civilization once spanned the world and was later erased. That claim is not what the maps themselves say.[[s:2]] Keeping those two things apart is most of the work.

The map shows a label. It does not show an empire.

Where the buildings come in

Many posts point to grand 19th-century buildings and temporary exhibition halls as evidence. Those structures have construction records, architects, and dates.[[s:3]] A world's fair, for example, put up large showpieces meant to be taken down. You can follow that paper trail yourself, and a map room is a good place to start.[[find:visit-an-old-map-room]]

What to do with a clip that says otherwise

Watch it, then ask what it cites. Does it point to a record you can open? Does it name the building, the year, the builder? A clip can be a good reason to look. It is a starting point, not a source.
