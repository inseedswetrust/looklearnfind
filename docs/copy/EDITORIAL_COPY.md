# LookLearnFind — editorial copy deck

Working copy for Claude. Bracketed fields require the founder's facts. These are page introductions and planning prompts, not reported claims.

## The editorial idea

Interesting ideas travel fast. The context that makes them useful often travels more slowly. LookLearnFind gives an idea three places to go: **Look** at a short post worth your attention, **Learn** through a deeper account with numbered source notes, and **Find** a person, place, guide, or product that lets you explore it in the world. The invitation is to stay curious, read the record, and make room for what is still unresolved.

## Four category introductions

### Politics

The decisions closest to home can be the hardest to follow. A ballot arrives with names, offices, proposals, and arguments that rarely fit in one post. Here we start with what is being asked, who has power to act, and where the public record begins. Follow a thread across a short clip, a sourced explanation, and the people or resources that can help you take the next step. Some questions have a clear answer. Others deserve a fuller account of what each choice would change.

Suggested threads:
- **The local ballot:** Offices, measures, and the decisions attached to each.
- **Following a promise:** What a candidate proposed, what the office can do, and what happened next.
- **Public money:** Where an allocation starts and how residents can trace it.

### Economy

A price, paycheck, or small business decision can look simple until you follow the pieces behind it. Economy collects the everyday evidence: receipts, costs, work, tradeoffs, and the rules that shape them. We look for useful explanations that connect the larger picture to choices people actually face, without pretending every household or business has the same room to maneuver. Start with a question you recognize, open the underlying material, then find the people, tools, or places that make the subject tangible.

Suggested threads:
- **The price on the shelf:** Costs and choices along a product's path to purchase.
- **Making a living:** How work, time, and income meet in different settings.
- **A business up close:** The decisions behind opening, operating, and staying useful.

### Society

How we live together shows up in ordinary places: a meeting, a street, a library, a group chat, a shared table. Society makes space for the ideas people bring into those places and the conditions that change what happens there. We follow the people and institutions involved, look for firsthand accounts and records, and stay alert to whose experience is missing. A good question here may lead to a useful conversation rather than a neat conclusion. That is a reason to keep looking.

Suggested threads:
- **The shared room:** What public spaces make possible and who can use them.
- **Belonging nearby:** How local groups form and sustain relationships.
- **A story in circulation:** What changes as a claim moves between communities.

### Everyday Life

Much of life is made of small experiments: a recipe that finally works, a room that needs fixing, a routine that fits the week you actually have. Everyday Life follows practical ideas without flattening them into universal advice. We ask what was tried, what it cost in time or money, and when a different approach would make more sense. Browse a short post, go deeper when the details matter, and find a guide, object, or local place that helps you try it yourself.

Suggested threads:
- **Make and mend:** Repairs, home projects, and practical learning curves.
- **Care in context:** Routines and products with room for different needs.
- **The second attempt:** What a project teaches after the first version.

## Twelve topic introductions and questions to bring

### Politics

Politics is easier to follow when a claim leads back to the decision it concerns. We gather useful posts, explain the relevant process, and point to the records behind the argument. Start with the office, proposal, or public meeting in question. Then ask what is settled, what remains open, and whose life the outcome will touch. The point is to enter the conversation with more context.

- What decision is actually on the table?
- Who can make or change it?
- Where is the public record?

### Candidates

A candidate is more than a clip or a campaign line. Look at the office they seek, the decisions it controls, their stated plans, and the record available to examine. We separate what a candidate says from what can be checked, and leave space for differences in priorities. Use this page to build a better set of questions before a forum, interview, or ballot.

- What can this office actually do?
- Which promises are specific enough to examine?
- What would you ask them directly?

### Businesses

Behind a storefront or an online checkout are choices about products, labor, service, and staying open. This topic follows the people making those choices and the details that help a customer understand them. A good business story shows the work, the constraints, and the experience of using what is offered. Browse with curiosity, then look for the questions a polished pitch leaves unanswered.

- What does this business actually make or do?
- What can a customer check firsthand?
- How does its model affect the people involved?

### Home improvement

A home project can begin with an appealing before-and-after, then turn into a list of materials, time, costs, and decisions. We collect ideas that show the process as well as the result. Look for the conditions a method depends on, the skill it requires, and the point at which a qualified professional belongs in the conversation. The useful detail is often what happened between the two photographs.

- What problem is the project solving?
- What tools, skills, or permissions are needed?
- Which costs and constraints were left out?

### Self improvement

An idea about changing a habit sounds different in the life of the person trying it. Here we look at routines, experiments, and questions about time, attention, and motivation with an eye for context. One person's useful practice may be another person's added pressure. Follow the method, notice what was measured or remembered, and decide which parts could fit your own circumstances.

- What would a realistic trial look like?
- What does this ask you to trade off?
- How would you know it helped?

### Side projects

The best side projects have a reason to exist beyond looking productive. This page follows small builds, tests, and creative efforts from the first question to the parts that got revised. We pay attention to materials, time, collaborators, and what a maker learned after showing the work to someone else. A useful example leaves enough detail for you to adapt the idea.

- What was the first workable version?
- Who tried it, and what changed?
- What would you make differently?

### Working out

Movement can be a practice, a social hour, a goal, or a way to feel more at home in a body. We look beyond the most dramatic clip to the exercise itself: how it is done, what it asks of someone, and how people adapt it. Follow sources when claims become specific. Keep the useful parts and leave room for different abilities, schedules, and preferences.

- What is the movement meant to do?
- How might it be adapted?
- Which claims need stronger evidence?

### Cooking

A good cooking post makes you want to start before the video ends. A good guide tells you enough to finish. We collect the ingredients, techniques, substitutions, and small observations that turn an appealing moment into a meal. Look for what changed in the pan, not just on the plate. There is room here for family knowledge, a weeknight shortcut, and the careful second attempt.

- Which step matters most?
- What can be substituted?
- How long does it really take?

### Supplements

Supplements invite confident promises and complicated labels. We approach them with a narrower question: what is the product, what is being claimed, and what kind of evidence would support that claim? A post can introduce an idea; it cannot establish a personal health outcome. Read the source and the label carefully, and bring questions about your circumstances to a qualified clinician when appropriate.

- What ingredient and amount are being discussed?
- What evidence supports the claimed effect?
- What personal considerations need professional advice?

### Skincare

Skincare is personal, visible, and crowded with persuasive stories. Here we slow down enough to distinguish an ingredient, a routine, an individual experience, and a broader claim. We look at how something is used, what it may cost, and how people with different skin or circumstances describe it. A useful find gives you a way to compare options without promising the same result for everyone.

- What exactly was used and how?
- Is the claim larger than the evidence?
- What would matter for your own skin?

### Self care

Care does not always look like a product or a perfectly planned morning. It may be a quieter hour, a needed appointment, a meal, a conversation, or help with a task. We collect concrete practices while asking what resources they take and whom they fit. The goal is to make a useful idea easier to examine and try, without turning a personal need into another standard to meet.

- What need does this address?
- What time or support does it require?
- How could it fit your actual week?

### Money

Money choices are shaped by numbers, goals, rules, and circumstances that differ from person to person. This topic makes the mechanics easier to see: what a term means, what a decision costs, and what assumptions an example makes. We show sources and avoid turning one person's approach into a prescription. Use the material to ask better questions before making a consequential choice.

- What assumptions drive the example?
- Which costs are easy to miss?
- What needs personal or professional review?

## Three editorial story briefs — reporting plans, not articles

### A first-time voter's local ballot

- **Question:** How can a first-time voter trace each local choice on a ballot to the office, proposal, and public record behind it?
- **Why now:** Tie publication to [ELECTION DATE AND LOCATION]; do not assert a race, candidate, deadline, or measure until verified.
- **Primary sources to seek:** Official sample ballot, election office instructions, candidate filings and statements, meeting records, budget and proposal text, direct interviews with relevant offices.
- **Finds:** Official voter resources, public forums, nonpartisan explainers whose methods can be checked, places to ask procedural questions.
- **Threads:** The local ballot; following a promise; public money.
- **Failure condition:** It mistakes campaign material for a neutral source, misses a deadline or eligibility nuance, or implies the publication endorses a candidate.

### What goes into a grocery price

- **Question:** What decisions and costs can be traced between a specific grocery item and its shelf price?
- **Why now:** Ground in [ITEM, LOCATION, OBSERVED DATE]; avoid implying a general trend from one receipt.
- **Primary sources to seek:** Itemized receipts, supplier or producer interviews, publicly available company disclosures, transport and labor records where obtainable, direct observation.
- **Finds:** Local market or producer, practical price-comparison guide, community food resource where relevant.
- **Threads:** The price on the shelf; making a living; a business up close.
- **Failure condition:** It treats a single price as a universal cost breakdown or omits an important intermediary.

### Comparing two home-renovation quotes

- **Question:** What can a homeowner compare when two quotes describe apparently similar work?
- **Why now:** Connect to [REAL PROJECT AND LOCATION] with owner permission; distinguish project-specific conditions.
- **Primary sources to seek:** Redacted written quotes, scope documents, permit or licensing guidance from the relevant authority, direct interviews with tradespeople and the homeowner.
- **Finds:** Quote-comparison checklist, local permit office, qualified professionals for the work involved.
- **Threads:** Make and mend; the second attempt; a business up close.
- **Failure condition:** It compares unequal scopes, exposes private pricing details without consent, or turns a specific example into universal legal or safety advice.
