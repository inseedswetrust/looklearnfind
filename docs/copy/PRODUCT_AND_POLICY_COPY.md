## Find Ledger contribution guidelines — approximately 150 words

Save a short post because it opens a useful question, shows a process clearly, or puts an idea in a place worth examining. Link the original post. Add a topic, the type of find, and a short answer to “Why was this worth keeping?” Name the specific moment or claim that caught your attention, and say what someone could look into next. A careful note is more helpful than a verdict.

Do not submit private information, harassment, impersonation, misleading metadata, illegal material, or a copy of a video you do not control. Do not suggest that an editor or contributor verified a claim unless the linked reporting actually did so. Disclose a connection to a person, product, or organization you submit. Editors may correct tags, merge duplicates, add context, or remove entries; changes have an audit trail. Public saves are opt-in and reviewed before appearing. Private saves remain private.

**A save is a recommendation to look, not a verification.**

### What happens here

**Look** begins with short posts that preview an idea worth the scroll. We link to the original creator and make it easy to hold onto a promising question.

**Learn** takes more time. Deep dives examine the question, give perspective room, and number their source notes so you can open the material behind a claim.

**Find** brings the idea into reach: a person to learn from, a place to visit, a guide to use, or a product to compare. The Ledger lets members keep useful posts and follow other people's public saves without handing the whole route to an algorithm.

### How we work

We distinguish what someone said from what the record supports. We show the source when we can, say what remains open, and correct a mistake in public. Contributors can suggest a post; editors review public entries, refine context, and keep an audit trail. [LINK TO DETAILED METHOD AND CORRECTIONS POLICY]

### Invitation

Start with a post. Take the longer route when something catches. Keep what proves useful, and share a question that deserves a closer look.

## Ten founder interview questions

1. Who founded LookLearnFind, when, and where? What background is relevant to this work?
2. What particular experience made the need for this site feel real?
3. Who is currently making editorial, design, product, and moderation decisions? Which roles are still planned?
4. Who legally owns the site and its content? Is there a parent organization?
5. How is the project funded today, and what revenue models are planned?
6. Do the founder, editors, or owners have political, commercial, or organizational affiliations readers should know about?
7. What is the editorial selection and fact-checking process for Look posts, Learn pieces, and Find entries?
8. What happens when a source contradicts a published claim or a reader reports an error?
9. What community behavior do you most want to encourage, and what would make a contribution unsuitable?
10. Which real people can be named and photographed on About, and what links or contact details can be published?
