## 1. The biggest spender lost (Senate) — recommended
**Central conflict:** a front-runner with a huge head start against a lieutenant governor whose campaign was nearly broke but whose backer controlled a PAC. **Surprising insight:** the winner's own campaign was outspent about 26 to 1 on ads (AdImpact: $1.1M against $28.9M), but a Pritzker-backed PAC supplied $14.4M of her $15.5M and a crypto PAC spent $9.0M against her: the story is who paid for what, not who spent most. **Hook:** "Krishnamoorthi spent $28.9 million on ads. Stratton's campaign spent $1.1 million. She won." **Sources:** AdImpact https://adimpact.com/blogs/open-illinois-congressional-seats-fuel-92m-in-primary-ad-spending · Capitol News Illinois https://capitolnewsillinois.com/news/stratton-defeats-krishnamoorthi-with-help-from-chicago-downstate-voters · The American Prospect https://prospect.org/2026/03/17/illinois-senate-primary-super-pac-spending-juliana-stratton-raja-krishnamoorthi/ (all opened). **Risks:** Fairshake's total ranges from $5.5M to nearly $10M by outlet; AdImpact includes reservations and stops at Mar 13; final shares unconfirmed; no causal claim. **Watchlist note:** the old lead (`background`: Pritzker and Duckworth endorsed the winner) is superseded; she is not a dark horse versus the establishment, the money is the story.

## 2. Ford and the $5 million (IL-07) — recommended
**Conflict:** the largest documented outside spender backed the runner-up while a crypto PAC attacked the winner. **Insight:** about $6.6M of outside money (UDP $4.7M for Conyears-Ervin, Fairshake $1.9M against Ford, per AdImpact) and the candidate it was spent against won with a plurality in a field of about 13. **Hook:** "A PAC spent nearly $5 million for the runner-up. La Shawn Ford won with 24 percent." **Sources:** Chicago Tribune via Yahoo https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/la-shawn-ford-declares-victory-023800604.html · AdImpact (above) · FEC committee page https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00799031/ (LLF de-duplicated total $4,968,500, Feb 10 to Mar 10) · Forest Park Review https://www.forestparkreview.com/2026/03/23/rory-hoskins-wins-forest-parks-congressional-vote/ (opened); WTTW https://news.wttw.com/2026/03/17/ford-conyears-ervin-tight-race-replace-danny-davis-illinois-7th-district-primary (403, summary only). **Risks:** do not use the Tribune's account of Ford's 2014 federal case without the court record; "AIPAC-aligned" is a characterization (FEC shows a committee name and ID); vote shares differ across counts. **Watchlist note:** the old lead stands only in part (C2 documented, C3 not met: Davis endorsed him); do not label him a dark horse versus the establishment.

## 3. The handoff (IL-04) — recommended
**Conflict:** a seat passes from a congressman to his aide with no contested primary, and the ballot rules treat likely challengers very differently. **Insight:** 697 signatures for a Democratic primary candidate against 10,816 for an independent (more than 15 times), and the Board removed two independents 8-0 after hearing officers found them 486 and 1,222 short. **Hook:** "697 signatures to run in the Democratic primary. 10,816 to run as an independent." **Sources:** Riverside-Brookfield Landmark https://www.rblandmark.com/2026/07/22/two-independents-stricken-from-4th-district-election-ballot/ · WBEZ https://www.wbez.org/government-politics/elections/2026/10/01/what-to-know-4th-congressional-district-patty-garcia-chris-getty-lupe-castillo-ed-hershey-us-rep-jesus-chuy-garcia · ABC7 (García's reasons) https://abc7chicago.com/amp/post/illinois-4th-congressional-district-rep-jess-chuy-garca-discusses-decision-not-run-reelection-anoint-chief-staff/18123005/ · ABC7 (lawsuit) https://abc7chicago.com/post/chicago-alderman-byron-sigcho-lopez-files-lawsuit-effort-get-ballot-replace-us-rep-jesus-chuy-garcia/19621519/ (all opened). **Risks:** lawsuit status unknown (docket unchecked); "coronation" is critics' word; no coordination claim is supported; García's reasons are his own statements; the unexplained House floor reprimand is not used.

## 4. AIPAC-backed winners on the November ballot (IL-08 and IL-02) — needs work
**Candidate:** Melissa Bean (IL-08; won the primary with about 32 percent over Junaid Ahmed at about 26.5, per a search summary of WTTW) and Donna Miller (IL-02). **Conflict:** the franchise's test case: candidates helped by AIPAC-linked groups who are still competing. **Insight:** AdImpact: Bean $4.6M including $4.2M from Elect Chicago Women, Protect Progress and New Democrat Majority; Miller $6.0M including $4.4M from Affordable Chicago Now (as of Mar 13). **Hook:** "Two of the four open Illinois seats went to candidates AIPAC-linked groups backed. Both are on the November ballot." **Sources:** AdImpact (opened); Common Dreams https://www.commondreams.org/news/aipac-spending-illinois (opened; says AIPAC-backed Bean and Miller won, cites Politico for about $22M of AIPAC-linked spending, names no groups); Newsweek https://newsweek.com/daniel-biss-wins-illinois-9th-district-primary-after-aipac-drops-millions-11693729 (opened; describes Elect Chicago Women as tied to AIPAC, attributing Axios). **Risks (why NOT_READY):** the link of each committee to AIPAC is press characterization; no organization filing or primary record has been opened; Affordable Chicago Now's link is not described by AdImpact at all; no candidate responses. **Open:** open Politico's $22M piece and Axios; read the committees' FEC statements of organization; request comment from Bean, Miller and AIPAC.

## 5. Biss and the AIPAC pivot (IL-09) — needs work
**Candidate:** Daniel Biss (won the Mar 17 primary with 29.5 percent at 91 percent counted, per Newsweek/AP). **Conflict:** AIPAC-linked groups first spent against Biss, then shifted to Kat Abughazaleh; Laura Fine, whom they backed, finished third. **Insight:** Newsweek (citing Axios): Elect Chicago Women about $2.8M for Fine and $1.3M against Biss, Chicago Progressive Partnership about $165,500 against Abughazaleh; AdImpact: $5.0M+ from Elect Chicago Women and EDW Action Fund for Fine and $1.0M from Chicago Progressive Partnership against Abughazaleh. **Hook:** "They spent millions against him, then against his rival. He won." **Risks (why NOT_READY):** the figures differ by outlet; the AIPAC link is Axios's and Punchbowl's characterization; final shares are not confirmed (one search summary even listed a different third place); Biss's statement about AIPAC is a post on X, not a document.
