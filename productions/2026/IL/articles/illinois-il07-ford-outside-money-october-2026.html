The result

La Shawn K. Ford, a state representative, finished first in the Democratic primary for the seat Rep. Danny Davis is leaving. In unofficial returns the Chicago Tribune had Ford at 23.1 percent with about 78 percent of votes counted, ahead of Melissa Conyears-Ervin at 19.6 and Anthony Driver at 11.5.[[s:1]] After the vote was nearly all counted, the Forest Park Review, citing the New York Times, put Ford at nearly 24 percent district-wide and Conyears-Ervin at over 20.[[s:4]] The numbers differ by count and are unofficial. Ford is the Democratic nominee in a heavily Democratic district.

He faces Republican Chad Koppie Sr. in November. Both are on TIP: La Shawn K. Ford and Chad Koppie Sr., in the Illinois 7th District race. Neither has a graded issue on TIP. Not graded is not a zero: Ford holds a state office, and TIP's six issues grade congressional acts.

The money

Outside groups spent heavily in the race. We keep three kinds of figures apart, because they measure different things.

AdImpact figures include reservations and are dated March 13, 2026; they stop four days before the vote. Results are unofficial returns and differ by count. The chart shows two sets of numbers side by side and does not say one caused the other. Sources: AdImpact; Chicago Tribune; Forest Park Review.[[s:2]][[s:1]][[s:4]]

What the FEC filings show. The United Democracy Project, committee C00799031, reported independent expenditures supporting Conyears-Ervin. LLF's count of those filings, with duplicates between 24/48-hour and periodic reports removed, is $4,968,500 for communications dated February 10 to March 10, 2026.[[s:3]] The filings identify the committee by name and ID.

What outlets describe. AdImpact calls UDP an "AIPAC-aligned PAC" and counted $4.7 million from it of the $5.5 million spent for Conyears-Ervin as of March 13.[[s:2]] The Tribune called it "AIPAC-affiliated."[[s:1]] Those are the outlets' characterizations. We did not find a UDP or AIPAC statement about this race, and we have not seen the committee's own description of its relationship to AIPAC in the filings we used.

What the ad tracker counted against Ford. AdImpact counted about $1.9 million from Fairshake, a crypto-industry PAC, in ads attacking Ford.[[s:2]] The Tribune reported that the ads attacked Ford and that he called them slanderous.[[s:1]] We do not describe the ads' content beyond that and do not state why Fairshake spent.

What AP reported for the whole primary. AP, in a report carried by WTTW, put the spending against Ford at nearly $2.5 million and noted that he won anyway.[[s:5]] That is larger than AdImpact's $1.9 million because AdImpact's count stops on March 13. Tech Influence Watch, which tracks outside spending, lists $2,423,870 in one view and $2,475,786 in another; the difference is unresolved, and we do not present either as a reconciled FEC total.[[s:6]] This spending was aimed at Ford in the Democratic primary. It was not part of Koppie's campaign, and we found no indication the two were coordinated.

These figures should not be added together. FEC independent expenditures, AdImpact's estimates (which include reservations) and AP's total cover different periods and count different things.

What the two nominees' own committees report

The outside money above went to or against candidates in the primary. The November nominees' own campaign finance filings are smaller and different in kind. Both are FEC figures through October 14, 2026.

La Shawn K. Ford (committee C00905893) reported $819,320.56 in receipts: $511,698.34 from individuals, $123,250 from political committees (15.04 percent of receipts) and $175,000 in loans from himself, which is the candidate's own money and not an outside donation. He reported $725,848.50 in disbursements and $93,472.06 cash on hand, with $175,000 in debts owed by the committee.[[s:7]] Committee money is not the same as corporate PAC money: it includes labor, trade, leadership and candidate committees, and we have not classified the 45 committees, so we give no corporate share. Ford's filings show both individual donors and committee backing.

Chad Koppie Sr. (committee C00911453) reported $60,408.81 in receipts, of which $55,951.51, or 92.62 percent, came from himself, in cash and in-kind costs. One other individual gave $2,118.75 in in-kind accounting and compliance services. The committee reported no party or PAC contributions and $42.20 cash on hand.[[s:8]] We found no outside spending for or against him in the FEC's independent expenditure records at the time we checked, which does not prove none was unreported.

The comparison is bounded. It shows where each campaign's reported money came from. It does not show that either campaign is or is not controlled by an interest, and funding alone does not show how anyone voted or will vote. The totals cover different periods: Koppie's committee was registered in July 2025.

Not a fight against the establishment

A story about a candidate who won against outside spending can drift into a story about an outsider beating the party. This one does not fit that frame. Davis, who is retiring, backed Ford and attended an early gathering of his supporters, the Tribune reported.[[s:1]] Ford won with a plurality of the vote in a crowded field, not a majority. The documented fact is the pairing of the two: the largest documented outside spender we found backed the runner-up, and the candidate a second outside group spent against finished first.

What this does not show

  • It does not show that spending raised or lowered anyone's vote. Judging that would take comparable polling and precinct data, and we do not have them.
  • It does not show who UDP's donors are or how it chose its candidate.
  • It does not describe anyone's campaign as grassroots or as backed by a special interest. The filings show where the reported money came from, nothing more.
  • It does not describe Ford's record or Conyears-Ervin's. Ford has no graded TIP issues, and the TIP page for the race shows who is on the November ballot.
